# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

- **Neptune North judges** at GreatUniHack (November 2026). They score four things from the sponsor brief: working towards a common goal, independent decision-making, resilience when communications fail, and something the team is excited about. They watch a 2-minute live demo standing around Laptop A's screen, up close.
- **ElevenLabs and n8n prize judges.** They need to see and hear their tool doing real work: the radio voices, and n8n receiving incidents, backlogs and the hospital pre-alert.
- **The team, operating it live.** Two people run the demo: they start the mission, cut links, put the phone in airplane mode, and watch device status while talking.

## Product Purpose

Blackout shows six rescue actors (four scouts, two rescuers) on three devices keeping one shared goal, reaching every survivor before their deadline, while their communication fails in stages. The HQ dashboard on Laptop A is the window into what HQ knows; the phone page is Scout 4, the actor that goes completely dark on stage. Success is a judge understanding, within seconds and without explanation, that actors kept working while cut off and that the system merged what they learned when links returned.

## Positioning

The failure in the demo is real and physical: a phone in airplane mode and a laptop whose radio is cut, not a simulated toggle on a slide. HQ's view is honestly partial: actors it cannot hear are drawn from their last report, and their work arrives late, in a burst, on reconnect. Gemini only chooses among options the rules already judged feasible, and every decision is labelled Gemini or Rules.

## Operating Context

- HQ dashboard: Laptop A's own screen (no projector), viewed up close by 2 to 4 judges, during a 2-minute demo plus Q&A. Must read at a glance with no scrolling on a laptop screen.
- Phone page: held up by a presenter, visible from about a metre; must be legible and unmistakable when it switches to "alone".
- Audio plays from Laptop A's speakers (HQ radio) and from the phone while it is cut off.
- n8n's Executions tab is shown separately to the n8n judges, with the dashboard's "Cut n8n link" control.
- Mission: 100 ticks at 1 tick per second, scripted faults (two links cut at t12, Laptop B radio off t28 to t72), phone airplane mode by hand.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Stack: TypeScript, Node 24 hub, esbuild bundles, plain HTML pages served by the hub. No framework.
- The dashboard only knows what reaches the hub; it must not show information HQ could not have.
- Terms: actor, scout, rescuer, survivor, tick, link, cut off, standing orders (written by Argon), backlog.
- Seeded 16x16 grid map with water tiles; survivors appear over time with deadlines.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: Blackout.
- The Neptune North brief ("Actors work better together") uses a mesh-graph illustration: navy nodes, light blue links, red crosses on cut links, one isolated node highlighted pink. The user asked to take inspiration from it while going beyond its simple abstraction.
- Writing voice: plain, direct, specific numbers, no em dashes, no AI-sounding phrases.

## Evidence on Hand

- Baseline over 50 seeds with the same fault timeline: mesh 92%, central control 68%, fully independent 58% of survivors reached (out/baseline.json, regenerated by `npm run baseline`).
- Live runs: Laptop B cut off at t28 and back at t72; 13 late reports delivered on reconnect; n8n executions for incident open/close, backlog and hospital pre-alert.
- No testimonials, users or deployments exist. Do not invent any.

## Product Principles

1. Show only what HQ can know; staleness is shown, never hidden.
2. Resilience must be visible in seconds: who is cut off, for how long, and what they did meanwhile.
3. Every decision is attributable: rules, Gemini, or standing orders.
4. The live failure is the hero; the interface supports it and never competes with it.
